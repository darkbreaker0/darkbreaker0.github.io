*A short, honest guide to how I think, communicate, and mentor — so we can work well together from day one. It's a living document; tell me where it's wrong, and we'll write the next edition together.*

## What I care about

- **Rigorous, mechanistic science.** I want to understand *how* things work, not just *that* they correlate — from molecules and forces up to circuits.
- **Curiosity over prestige.** The question and the craft come first. Good work, done carefully, tends to find its audience.
- **Independence.** My aim is to help you become a scientist who no longer needs me — who can frame a question, build the tools, and defend the answer.
- **Kindness and honesty.** A lab is people first. I'd rather hear the hard thing early, kindly, than discover it late.
- **A bit of humour.** Science is long and uncertain; a lab that laughs together lasts.

## How I think about science

I'm a cell biologist and biochemist by training who became fascinated by how the nervous system builds itself. I work at the intersection of the **cytoskeleton, cell mechanics, and neurodevelopment**, and I like to pair **quantitative analysis and modelling with hands-on experiments** — reductionist when a mechanism needs isolating, holistic when a system needs to be seen whole. I think in **images and data**: a good visualization often *is* the insight, and I lean on programming as an everyday tool for asking biological questions.

## How we'll communicate

- **Weekly written updates beat rigid calendars.** I've learned about myself that a short weekly note — what I tried, what I found, what's next — keeps momentum and gives us both a real sense of progress. Minute-by-minute scheduling tends to demotivate me, and I assume the same may be true for you. We'll plan around **milestones and week-by-week tasks**, and review the pace together, kindly.
- **My door is open.** For anything time-sensitive or worrying, don't wait for the weekly note — come find me.
- **I think out loud.** Early ideas from me are invitations to argue, not instructions. Push back.

## Feedback — giving and getting

- I give **frequent, small, concrete feedback** rather than rare verdicts. It's about the work, never the person.
- I **want your feedback too** — on the project, on my mentoring, on the lab. I take it seriously and I won't be offended.
- If something I did landed badly, tell me. I'd much rather fix it than have you carry it.

## How I mentor — and what I hope to give you

- **Skills that travel.** I'll help you build quantitative and computational muscle — coding, data analysis, visualization — and I actively encourage using modern tools to learn faster. Watching a trainee pick up coding from scratch and start analysing their own data is the most satisfying part of the job.
- **Room to own a project**, with guardrails so you don't get lost — scaled to your stage.
- **Career support that's honest.** I've navigated the job market myself, including its setbacks, and I'll be candid about trade-offs, networking, and timing. I'll spend my capital opening doors for you.
- **An interdisciplinary view.** I like learning across fields and will push us to borrow ideas from physics, computation, and neuroscience.

## What I'll expect from you

- **Ownership and honesty** — especially about what *isn't* working. Blockers shared early are cheap; hidden ones are expensive.
- **Care and rigour** in the data, the records, and with each other.
- **Curiosity and initiative** — bring questions, not just results.
- **Look out for your labmates.** We rise together.

## My quirks and growth edges (so you're not surprised)

- I'm **continually working on concise writing and realistic timelines** — I tend to underestimate how long things take, so we'll plan with buffer and revise estimates openly.
- I get visibly happy about an elegant figure or a clean analysis.
- I'm **reflective and self-critical**; if I go quiet, I'm usually thinking, not unhappy — but feel free to check.
- I value a warm, low-ego lab. Cake on your first day is encouraged. 🙂

## What you can always expect from me

Time and attention; honest, kind guidance; advocacy for your career; and a genuine stake in your growth as a scientist and as a person.
